# Direction and musical storytelling

## Read first

Source priorities: (1) user instructions and supplied lyric/master, (2) verified media properties and source footage, (3) inferred creative direction. Never fabricate a scene that depends on a nonexistent clip.

## Timing model

Separate four concepts: section boundaries, musical accents, sung word/phrase onsets, and desired visual cut points. They can differ. Use seconds derived from the real master. Store estimated boundaries with `confidence: "unverified"` until checked.

## Narrative design

For each section identify: storyline event; lyric cue; dominant asset; symbol; point of audience attention; emotional direction; transition into the next scene. The visual spine should progress as the song progresses. Where lyrics depict a place, object or character, prioritize truthful symbolism over generic unrelated stock shots.

## Intensity ladder

- Low: reveal a key visual clearly and hold it for comprehension.
- Medium: layered composition and camera motion, with legible lyrics.
- High: multiple synchronized effects, motivated by narrative beats.
- Extreme: brief, deliberate screen-filling graphic impact in nonessential negative space or clear cut points.

Escalation is not equivalent to permanent maximal density. A moment of visual stillness gives contrast to an explosion of motion.

## Artifacts

- `analysis/creative-brief.md`: concept, emotional arc, motifs, treatment, palette, forbidden elements.
- `analysis/timing.json`: audio master duration, verified sections, lyric cue and confidence.
- `analysis/shot-plan.md`: ranges, assets, shots, VFX, text and checkpoints.
- `analysis/asset-ledger.csv`: file, source, licensing, usage and attribution.
